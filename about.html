A little about me

I'm Peter Musa, a Computer Science student at Miva Open University, and a digital artist and graphic designer from Jos, Nigeria. I'm using this chapter of my life to build technical skills on top of the creative foundation I already have.

I've loved tech and creativity from a tender age. As a kid, I would quietly pick up my mom's phone at night while she slept, and later my siblings' phones too, just to explore and play games. They would wake up to new changes on their phones, because I loved customizing things. I was curious about everything on those screens, and I wanted to know how it all worked. Growing up playing video games turned that curiosity into a dream: to dive into game development and create worlds, characters, and stories that give other kids the same excitement those late nights gave me.

Today, that dream guides my path. I'm interested in more than just using technology. I want to build it: to take an idea, sketch it, code it, make mistakes, fix them, and eventually create something people enjoy. My path leads toward full stack development, UI/UX design, and game development.

I'm still learning, and that's part of the point. This website is a snapshot of my journey: what I'm learning, what I'm creating, and who I'm becoming along the way. They call me Pe6tens, which stands for Persistence, because I keep digging deeper until there's room to flourish.

Why Pe6tens?

Pe6tens stands for Persistence. Not the loud kind that argues over closed doors, but the quiet discipline that moves forward and builds again.

The idea comes from the story of Isaac from the Holy Bible in Genesis 26. He dug wells in dry land, and the herdsmen of Gerar quarreled over the first two. He named them Esek (Dispute) and Sitnah (Opposition), then moved on and dug again. He didn't waste his strength on pointless battles, and he didn't quit. He kept digging until he reached a well nobody contested, and he named it Rehoboth, meaning "room".

That's how I approach life, learning and creative work. When a path gets blocked or an effort gets disrupted, I adapt, start fresh and keep digging until I find room to flourish. Because Persistence breaks Resistance.
